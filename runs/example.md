## EVALUATION: "Airbnb for GPUs" — a marketplace for spare GPU compute between indie game studios and small AI labs

**Date:** 2026-09-20
**Source:** orders/example.md
**Pitch as I read it:** A two-sided marketplace that aggregates idle GPUs from people who own hardware and rents them by the hour to small AI teams below cloud-provider prices, monetized with a 10–15% take rate.

### Niche

- **Category:** Two-sided GPU compute rental marketplace — a broker of third-party accelerator capacity. The mechanics (aggregate supply you don't own, schedule jobs onto it, hold payment, score reputation, clip a take rate) are marketplace mechanics, not game-industry or AI-tooling mechanics.
- **Nearest substitutes:** (1) peer-to-peer GPU marketplaces — Vast.ai, Salad, TensorDock, Render Network; (2) "neocloud" hourly GPU renters — RunPod, Lambda, CoreWeave, Prime Intellect; (3) aggregators sitting *above* the marketplaces — Shadeform, Compute Exchange; (4) hyperscaler on-demand/spot — AWS, Azure, GCP; (5) free defaults — Colab free tier, Kaggle weekly GPU hours; (6) the manual workaround — buy a used consumer card and put it under the desk.
- **Buyer:** The demand side — a solo ML developer or a small AI team paying per GPU-hour out of a discretionary infra budget. The GPU owner is supply, not the payer; a studio deciding whether to list a workstation is deciding whether to accept money, not whether to spend it.
- **Vs. the pitch's own framing:** The pitch frames a community of two peers ("studios and labs trade with each other") and names the innovation as the matching. The market's frame is blunter: this is the GPU rental marketplace category, which has had named incumbents since 2018, and where the product is liquidity, not matching software. Mine is the real one because every mechanic in the pitch — take rate, reputation, scheduling — is a marketplace mechanic, and marketplaces are evaluated against the liquidity that already exists, not against the code needed to run them. One refinement the searches added rather than contradicted: indie game studios do appear on the *demand* side too, for rendering — and that demand is served by render-farm incumbents (Render Network, iRender) rather than by AI GPU clouds.
- **Corrected mid-run?** No. Both hostile searches confirmed the category (GPU rental marketplace), the substitute set (P2P marketplaces, neoclouds, aggregators, free tiers, buy-your-own), and the buyer (the per-GPU-hour renter).

### Checks

- **Timing:** Window derived from lenses 1, 3 and 4 — **closed, not open**. Lens 3 says the capability has worked since roughly 2018 for exactly the workloads consumer GPUs can carry, so "obvious" happened eight years ago; lens 1 says the price curve for that tier has since bent down and the provider field consolidated from 100+ to under 40; lens 4 says the remaining loudness is narrative, not readiness. One honest counterweight: compute is not uniformly cheap — H100 contract pricing rose ~40% from Oct 2025 to Mar 2026 and on-demand rates recently ticked up 15–20% on HBM shortages ([shattered.io](https://shattered.io/h100-h200-b200-cloud-gpu-pricing-2026/), 2026) — but that scarcity is in frontier datacenter silicon this pitch has no access to, not in the idle consumer cards it would aggregate. Can you ship inside the window? You could ship marketplace *software* in 3–6 months at 3h/day; you cannot ship liquidity, which is what the window would have required. **Too late.**
- **Incumbent:** *searched: "Vast.ai RunPod Salad decentralized GPU marketplace rent idle GPUs price per hour 2026" (P2P marketplaces + neoclouds), "Salad Cloud rent out idle gaming GPU earnings host payout" (consumer idle-GPU supply), "Vast.ai host commission take rate" (marketplace economics), "Shadeform GPU cloud aggregator broker single API" (the aggregation layer above), "indie game studios idle workstation GPUs render farm rental" (creative/render substitutes), "rent GPU vs buy Colab Pro free tier what indie developers actually use 2026" (free defaults + buy-your-own)* → Vast.ai sells this exact product at $0.51/hr for an RTX 4090 and $1.49–1.73/hr for an H100 across 17,000+ GPUs and 500+ locations ([vast.ai/pricing](https://vast.ai/pricing), [gpuperhour.com](https://gpuperhour.com/), Sep 2026); RunPod sells the managed version at $0.69/hr (4090) and $2.89/hr (H100 PCIe) ([runpod.io](https://www.runpod.io/articles/guides/top-cloud-gpu-providers), 2026); Salad sells the idle-consumer-PC version and pays hosts in redeemable balance ([community.salad.com](https://community.salad.com/sell-gpu-power/)); Shadeform sells the layer above, auto-routing to the cheapest of 30+ clouds through one API ([shadeform.com](https://shadeform.com/)); Colab free and Kaggle's weekly GPU hours cover the notebook tier at $0; and a used RTX 3090 at $850–$1,200 covers sustained use outright ([codersera.com](https://codersera.com/blog/cheapest-way-to-run-local-llm-2026/), 2026) → Why won't they do it, or do it faster? They did it first — Vast.ai and Salad both since 2018 — and the reputation data that makes a marketplace work is precisely what eight years buys: new hosts earn 30–40% of steady-state in month one because rank is built from uptime history ([earnifyhub.com](https://earnifyhub.com/learning-guides/make-money-renting-out-gpu-2026), 2026). The take-rate arithmetic makes the gap concrete: at 10–15% of a $0.44–$0.69 consumer GPU-hour you clear $0.04–$0.10 per hour sold, so a $2,000/month business needs on the order of 30,000 billed GPU-hours — roughly 40–60 consumer GPUs rented continuously — against Salad's 450,000 and Vast.ai's 17,000+. · **durable** (durably against the pitch: the block is the incumbents' accumulated liquidity and reputation history, not a temporary "they haven't yet")
- **Reality:** *searched: "how much do small AI startups solo developers spend per month on GPU compute rental budget survey 2026"* → They pay, and out of an operating budget: GPU compute runs 40–60% of technical budgets in an AI startup's first two years, with early-stage teams at $2,000–$8,000/month in prototype and $10,000–$30,000/month in production, and solo developers at roughly $50–$500/month for part-time use of a single A100/4090-class instance ([gmicloud.ai](https://www.gmicloud.ai/en/blog/how-much-do-gpu-cloud-platforms-cost-for-ai-startups-in-2026), 2026; [thundercompute.com](https://www.thundercompute.com/blog/ai-gpu-rental-market-trends), Sep 2026). Note these are provider-blog and VC figures rather than survey-grade data — directional, not precise. Supply-side money is real too: consumer cards earn hosts $0.30–$0.60/GPU-hour at 65–85% utilization ([vast.ai](https://vast.ai/article/how-much-money-can-you-earn-renting-out-your-gpu-on-vast-ai)). · *capability today:* It works, at scale, for the workloads consumer hardware can hold — batch inference, rendering, fine-tuning, hyperparameter sweeps, at 30–50% below centralized cost — and does not work for long-duration frontier training, which needs homogeneous low-latency clusters ([spheron.network](https://www.spheron.network/blog/decentralized-llm-training-pluralis-prime-intellect-nous-psyche/), 2026). This is the one criterion the pitch wins outright.
- **Builder-fit:** **Column A — hit, but by a barrier that stands against you rather than for you.** The run evidences real build difficulty, and the incumbent search is the telling part: what it took the existing players is 1,400+ provider relationships, 500+ locations, an automated continuous verification algorithm, SOC 2 Type II, multi-rail host payouts (Stripe/Wise/PayPal), and eight years of uptime history to rank hosts by. Lens 6 confirms the barrier's nature — trust and reputation accumulation, the kind you can't buy — and lens 2 shows the adjacent high-ground (routing, brokerage, hedging) already taken by funded teams. **Column B — failed, and inverted.** For a bootstrapper the out-resourcer is venture money, and this is among the most capital-contested segments in AI infrastructure: 100+ VC-funded neoclouds, a YC company at the aggregation layer, and CME/ICE building futures markets on top. The question "who could crush this builder by simply showing up?" has an unusually crowded answer, and about sixty of them already showed up and died of margin compression. **Edge crossing Column A: none.** Ten years of full-stack TypeScript/Java/Python builds the software, which is not the hard part; embeddings, vector search and RAG are unrelated to GPU scheduling and host verification; AWS Solutions Architect and AI Practitioner certify consuming a hyperscaler, not operating a fleet of untrusted third-party hosts. The order states outright there's no deep domain knowledge of a specific industry's pains — in a market whose barrier is relationships with GPU owners, that's the missing edge named by the order itself.

### Verdict: LOW · 2/10

The product in this pitch already exists twice over and has since 2018 — Salad for idle consumer GPUs (450,000+ contributed, auto-pausing when the owner games) and Vast.ai for the marketplace itself (17,000+ GPUs, 1,400+ providers) — while the price of the thing you'd take 10–15% of has fallen to roughly $0.44–$0.51 per GPU-hour, leaving about five cents per hour sold to fund a business against incumbents with eight years of liquidity.

**Gate fired:** three lenses returned `kills` on three separate facts, at least one `strong` — the bent price curve and provider shakeout (lens 1), the registered-vs-active gap in decentralized compute (lens 4), and the incumbents already shipping this exact product (lens 6). Two would have been enough to cap the verdict at LOW. The Incumbent = 0 gate (MEDIUM cap) also fired and is subsumed by it.

### What to test — weakest criterion: Incumbent

Four criteria tied at 0 (Lenses, Timing, Incumbent, Builder-fit); the tie breaks toward the money, and since Reality scored 2, Incumbent is the binding constraint. The only version of this pitch that survives is one that finds demand the existing platforms *structurally cannot serve* — so test for that exclusion directly, before writing any code.

- **Experiment:** Be the marketplace manually for two weeks — no product. Contact 15 people who rented GPU time last month (r/LocalLLaMA, the RunPod and Vast.ai Discords, indie-gamedev and ML Slack groups) and ask one question: *what did you try to run, and what couldn't you run where you're running it?* Offer to source compute for them yourself, quoting a price and fulfilling it by hand on Vast.ai/RunPod at a 10% margin. In parallel, ask 10 studios or hardware owners whether they'd list a workstation they are *not* already listing, and why they haven't.
- **Time & cost:** ~20 hours over 2 weeks + under $100 (a few GPU-hours to fulfil an order, no domain, no code).
- **Success threshold:** 5 of the 15 name a concrete job they could not run on Vast.ai, RunPod or Salad for a structural reason that is **not** price (compliance, dual-use hardware, a scheduling shape nobody supports, data they can't put on a stranger's machine) — **and** at least 2 pay a real invoice of $50+ for you to broker it by hand. Because the real build is a two-sided liquidity problem you can't unwind, "that sounds useful" counts for nothing here; money or a signed commitment or it failed.
- **Fail is:** 15 people reached and every complaint is price, or nobody pays — which means you'd be entering as the cheapest option in a market whose cheapest option is already $0.44/hour and eight years old.
