∑ Pitch Polynomial

Six lenses look for a market error. Four checks ask whether it's yours.
The number says how much to believe it.

You already have a product or business idea. This framework tells you where it breaks, with sources, and what your first step is tomorrow. It's one self-contained prompt for any LLM with live web search.

How it works

1 · Niche

Before any search, it works out where the pitch actually competes, based on how it works rather than how you framed it: the category, the nearest substitutes (including free defaults and manual workarounds), and who actually pays.

2 · 6 lenses

Each lens asks one question, runs its own web search, and has a kill condition. The result is one finding per lens: a claim, its source, and a verdict of supports, neutral or kills.

3 · 4 checks

Timing, incumbent, reality and builder-fit. Two of them run hostile searches: who already sells this, at what price and who pays for this today, how much.

4 · Score

Five criteria, 0–2 each, out of 10. Gates cap the verdict regardless of the total. The lowest-scoring criterion becomes the two-week experiment to run next, with a cost, a success threshold and what counts as failure.

5 · Tomorrow

Your first step, tomorrow. Every verdict ends with one concrete thing to do the next day, like a message to send or a page to put up. It won't be a strategy, and it's the fastest way to learn whether the verdict holds.

The six lenses

  1. Linearization trap. Does the curve this pitch rides actually grow the way the pitch assumes?
  2. Complexity / emergence. Is there a genuinely new class of adaptive behaviour here, or a stable problem in new clothes?
  3. Paradigm blindness. Is the pitch on the right side of what experts are over-certain about, in either direction?
  4. Narrative error. Where does this market's story sit relative to what it actually delivers?
  5. The ostrich paradox. Is this a problem people want solved, or one they've quietly learned to live with?
  6. Organizational blindness. What stops the obvious player from doing this, structurally rather than temporarily?

The score

It measures conviction, not probability: how much the evidence supports taking the bet. The five criteria are lenses, timing, incumbent, reality and builder-fit.

HIGH 8–10MEDIUM 5–7LOW ≤4
nobody pays for this problem in any form → LOW
two or more lenses kill, on separate facts → LOW
the incumbent ships it as a feature   → MEDIUM at best
the niche couldn't be settled         → MEDIUM at best

Use it

With any LLM that has live web search. Paste the full pitch-polynomial.md as the system prompt or first message, then fill in five fields:

Pitch: A marketplace where indie game studios and small AI labs can rent
  spare GPU compute from each other, like Airbnb for GPUs
Edges: 10 years full-stack, hands-on with embeddings/RAG, AWS certified
Horizon: 3–6 months to MVP
Risk appetite: HIGH
Constraints: solo dev, ~$1,000 budget

With Claude Code. Clone the repo and use the skills:

/pitch-orderasks a few questions and writes an order
/pitch-run orders/<file>.mdruns the evaluation and writes the verdict
/pitch-run-review runs/<file>.mdindependently audits a finished run

Use a frontier model: the process is only as rigorous as the model running it. Any part of a run can be wrong and still look right, so review the output.

Experimental, not validated. This framework finds weaknesses in your idea; it doesn't predict success. It's not business, financial, investment or legal advice. The output is an AI-generated hypothesis and may be incomplete, outdated or wrong. Use it at your own risk.
